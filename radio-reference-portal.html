// Marine VHF · USA & Hawaii

Marine VHF Channels

Commonly monitored frequencies for recreational boaters, commercial traffic, harbor operations, Coast Guard, and NOAA weather in US and Hawaii waters.

📡

Always Monitor CH 16

156.800 MHz is the international distress and calling frequency. All VHF-equipped vessels must monitor it at all times.

🌊

Working Channels

Establish contact on a hailing channel, then move to a working channel. Keeps hailing channels clear for emergencies.

🌺

Hawaii Notes

CH 14 (156.700 MHz) is used by Honolulu VTS. WX1 (162.550 MHz) is typically the clearest NOAA signal on Oahu.

// Amateur Radio · HAM

HAM Radio Frequencies

Key amateur radio frequencies across HF, VHF, and UHF bands. FCC license required to transmit. Anyone may listen.

📻

Licensing

Three tiers: Technician, General, Extra. Each grants more band privileges. Get licensed at arrl.org.

🔁

Repeaters

VHF/UHF repeaters extend range dramatically. Most require a CTCSS/PL tone. Hawaii has an active network island-wide.

🌍

HF Propagation

HF bands (3–30 MHz) bounce off the ionosphere for worldwide contacts. Conditions vary with solar activity and time.

// Satellite & Space

Satellite Frequencies

Satellite and space-related frequencies monitorable with consumer radios and SDR receivers. No license required to listen.

📡

SDR Recommended

A $25 RTL-SDR dongle covers most of these. Use SDR# or GQRX. A V-dipole antenna is enough for 137 MHz satellites.

🌍

APT Weather Images

NOAA satellites broadcast analog images at 137 MHz. Receive and decode live weather photos with free software.

🚀

Doppler Shift

ISS moves at 7.7 km/s — causes ±3.5 kHz shift on 145 MHz. Gpredict can auto-correct during a pass.

// Aviation & Airband

Aviation Frequencies

Civil and military aviation uses AM on 118–136.975 MHz. Monitor only — transmitting requires an aeronautical license.

🛫

AM Modulation

Aviation uses AM, not FM. Ensure your receiver supports AM for the airband — most SDRs and scanners do.

🗼

Honolulu Airport

HNL Tower: 118.100 MHz · Ground: 121.900 · Approach: 119.100 · ATIS: 127.900. Verify with current NOTAMs.

⚠

Guard — 121.500

International emergency frequency. All military aircraft monitor continuously. Also used for ELT beacon detection.

// NOAA Weather Radio

NOAA Weather Broadcasts

NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards broadcasts continuous forecasts, warnings, and emergency alerts 24/7. No license required to receive.

🌦

WX1 — Oahu Primary

162.550 MHz is typically the clearest signal in the Honolulu/Oahu region. First channel to check in Hawaii waters.

🔔

SAME Alerts

NOAA uses SAME encoding to broadcast county-targeted warnings. Radios with SAME can filter for your area.

📻

All Hazards

Not just weather — NOAA WR also carries AMBER alerts, Presidential EAS alerts, and local emergency notifications.

// Digital Modes & Data

Digital Radio Modes

Digital signals sound like noise to the ear but carry structured data. Most can be decoded with free software and an SDR or audio cable.

📶

FT8 — Weak Signal

Decodes 20 dB below noise floor. Wildly popular on HF — thousands of stations active worldwide at any moment.

📍

APRS — Position Data

GPS positions, weather data, messages over packet radio. 144.390 MHz is the North American network. Track at aprs.fi.

🚢

AIS — Vessel ID

Ships broadcast ID, position, course, speed on 161.975 and 162.025 MHz. Decode with any SDR + OpenCPN.
