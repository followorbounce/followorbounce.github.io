Video Synth · Instrument

Upside Down

Beams of light come down from above and meet water. At the surface each one splits: part of it bends into the water and fades with depth, and part of it bounces back as a reflection. The reflection is a mirror that trembles: touch the water and rings spread across it, bending every reflected beam. Turn on Sound and each beam becomes a voice, detuned by the waves at the point where it meets the water.

Light
Stage

Sound: one sine voice per beam on a pentatonic scale; the waves at each beam's landing point bend its pitch.

Press or drag on the stage to drop ripples · lower on the stage is nearer to you on the water · Rain drops ripples on its own · Sound starts only when you press it.

How it works

The water. The surface is a 256 × 128 height field simulated with the discrete wave equation, two textures taking turns: h′ = (sum of the four neighbours)/2 − h_prev, times a damping just under one. A drop adds a small Gaussian bump; the equation turns it into rings that spread, reflect at the edges and die away. The field lies on the water plane seen at a low angle: rows just below the waterline are far away and rows at the bottom of the stage are close, so ripples flatten into ellipses towards the horizon.

Splitting the light. Above the water, each beam is a line from its emitter to its landing point, glowing in a little haze. Below, two things happen. The refracted beam carries on into the water, bent towards the local surface normal by Snell's law with water's index of 1.33, and fades with depth as Clarity allows (Beer–Lambert, e^(−k·d)), shifting towards blue-green. The reflection is the scene above, mirrored at the waterline and displaced by the slope of the waves at each point, so every ripple bends every reflected beam at once.

Sound. Each beam is a sine voice on a pentatonic scale, quiet and sustained. The height of the water where a beam lands bends that voice's pitch up or down by up to a semitone, so a ripple passing under the fan plays across the voices in the order it reaches them.

Lineage

A study in the language of light and medium: beams made visible by what they pass through, water and reflection multiplying them downwards, and the viewer's touch as the disturbance. It recreates no specific work.

Limits

  • This is a picture model, not optics: there is one bounce, no caustics on a floor, and the reflection is of the beams only.
  • The wave equation runs at a fixed resolution, so very small ripples on very large screens look soft.
  • The waves need a device that can render to floating-point textures; where it can't, the water stays calm and the mirror still works.
  • No flashing: drops make rings that spread, not bursts of light. Reduced motion slows everything to 30 %.
