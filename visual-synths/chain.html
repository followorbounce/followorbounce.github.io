Video Synth · Instrument

Chain

Point a camera at its own screen and the image falls into itself: the oldest trick in video art, and the root of most live visuals. Here the loop is opened up. Every frame the last frame goes back through a chain of passes, each a small full-screen shader, and a new shape is drawn on top. Change the order and the same passes make a different image. Every pass says what it does and shows its line of GLSL. Press and drag on the stage to paint into the loop.

The chain

    Passes run top to bottom, every frame, on the previous frame. ↑ and ↓ move a pass, × removes it, ? shows what it does. Up to eight passes. Press and drag on the stage to paint.

    How it works

    A loop of textures. Two half-float textures take turns: one holds the last frame, the other receives the next. Each pass in the chain is one full-screen draw that reads a texture and writes the next. After the last pass, the source shape (and anything you paint) is drawn over the result, and that becomes the frame the next loop starts from. What you see is the loop's current state, shown as it is.

    Why order matters. The passes don't commute. A blur before a threshold makes soft blobs crisp; a threshold before a blur makes crisp shapes soft. A small zoom before a fade makes a tunnel whose walls dim with depth; a fade before a hue shift makes every generation a new colour. Five presets show classic orders. Change one pass and you get a new instrument.

    Stability. Feedback multiplies whatever is in the loop every frame, so any pass that adds energy (an edge filter, a sharp threshold) needs a Fade somewhere to keep the image from saturating to white. Half-float textures keep slow fades smooth instead of banding into steps.

    Lineage

    A study in the language of image-pass systems: complex effects built only from operations on whole images, in the tradition of analogue video feedback, and the habit of opening the machinery so anyone can learn from it. It recreates no specific work.

    Limits

    • Melt is an approximation of pixel sorting: it drags the brightest value downwards over a fixed reach instead of sorting columns.
    • Strong feedback can build to bright, fast-changing images. Changes are capped so the whole frame cannot flash more than three times a second, and reduced motion slows the loop.
    • No camera or video input yet; the sources are drawn shapes and your painting.
