Threshold

An invisible field rises and falls under the screen. You only see where it crosses a level: thin bright bands, like the contour lines of weather. Press and drag to pile pressure into the field and watch the bands bend around your hand. Trigger events to accumulate, saturate and release. Where the field saturates, it turns red. Turn on Sound and the drone follows the same numbers: pitch from the threshold, brightness from the pressure, a burst of noise on every saturation.

Events
Palette

Press and drag on the stage to add pressure · keys A accumulate, S saturate, R release, C clear · Sound starts only when you press it.

How it works

A field and a level. The body of the image is fractal noise (five octaves of value noise) drifting slowly, plus a pressure map you paint. Nothing of the field itself is drawn. A pixel lights up only where the field crosses the threshold or of one of the levels stacked above and below it, so you see contour lines, the way a weather map shows pressure. Each band is measured in screen pixels (the distance to the level divided by how fast the field changes across one pixel), so a band keeps the Band width you set wherever the field is steep or flat.

Pressure. Pressing paints a soft Gaussian into a half-resolution buffer that is read back every frame and multiplied by a decay factor, p ← p·e^(−k·dt) + brush. The bands bend around what you paint and relax as it fades. Accumulate lifts the whole field for a few seconds, Release drains the pressure map faster, and Saturate pushes a second, finer noise field over its own threshold so the hottest cells turn red, then lets it fall back.

Weather. With Weather on, the threshold drifts on two slow, unrelated sine cycles, so bands appear, merge and vanish without repeating for a long time. The grain is renewed every frame and is stronger where the image is bright, like film. Sound is three detuned sawtooth oscillators through a low-pass filter: the threshold sets the pitch, total pressure opens the filter, and every Saturate fires a band of noise. There are no samples.

Lineage

A study in the language of threshold systems: rules, levels and saturation instead of drawn forms, a palette of black, grey and red, grain as surface, and sound made from the same events as the image. It recreates no specific work.

Limits

  • The field is noise, not a physical model: pressure doesn't flow or conserve, it only adds and fades.
  • Saturate brightens red cells over about a second and fades them over three; it can't be triggered faster than three times a second, so nothing flashes.
  • On very large screens the pressure buffer is at half resolution, so painted edges are slightly soft.
