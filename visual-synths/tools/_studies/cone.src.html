Cone

A black room, a little haze, and lasers on rotating mounts. Each beam is a line of light; turn it around a vertical axis and it sweeps a cone, stand it upright on a turning ring and it sweeps a cylinder, rock it and it opens a fan. The haze is what makes light visible: where it thickens, the beams burn brighter; where it thins, they almost vanish. The piece drifts through phases, from a slow, narrow calm to fast, wide intensity, the way weather does. Drag to walk around the room.

How it works

Beams as lines, light as distance. There is no geometry. For every pixel the shader casts a ray from the eye and, for each beam, finds the point where the ray passes closest to the beam's segment (two skew lines have one closest pair of points, found in closed form). The light reaching the eye is a narrow Gaussian of that distance, times the haze density at that point, times a slow fall-off along the beam. A beam's width is never allowed to drop below the size of a pixel at that depth, and its brightness is scaled down by the same factor, so far beams stay smooth instead of shimmering.

Sweeps. A Cone mount turns a beam tilted by the Opening angle around a vertical axis, so the beam sweeps a cone whose tip is the emitter. A Cylinder keeps every beam vertical and turns the whole ring of emitters. A Fan rocks each beam back and forth in one plane. Where a beam meets the floor it leaves a spot; the spots trace circles, ellipses and lines that show the sweep from above.

Haze and weather. The haze is two octaves of 3D value noise drifting on a slow wind, so beams brighten and fade as they pass through denser air. Weather moves the piece between calm (slow turning, narrow openings, thin haze) and intense (fast, wide, dense) on a cycle of about a minute, with smooth transitions. Turn Weather off to hold a phase by hand. The grain is per-pixel noise renewed every frame and kept faint, like film stock.

Lineage

A study in the language of laser architecture: light as the only material, a restricted palette, mechanical rotation turned into volumes of light, and systems that move through phases instead of loops. It recreates no specific installation.

Limits

  • Single scattering only: light is not bounced from haze to haze, and beams do not cast shadows on each other.
  • The beams are lines with no divergence; real laser beams spread slightly and speckle.
  • No flashing by design: brightness changes come from continuous motion. With reduced motion set, everything runs at 30 % speed.
  • Twelve beams at full-screen 4K is heavy; the study renders at a capped resolution and keeps the frame rate by lowering it further if needed.
