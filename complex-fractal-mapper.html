Instrument · Complex Dynamics

Complex-Plane Fractal Mapper

One recurrence, z → zpower + c, iterated at every point of the complex plane until it escapes or it doesn't. Where it doesn't escape is the Mandelbrot or Julia set; where — and how fast — it does is colored with Palette Synth's own cosine-gradient formula. Pan, zoom, and change the exponent live — or just drag directly on the picture.

What this actually is

  
Pick a point c in the complex plane, start with z = 0, and repeat z = zpower + c. For most starting points z shoots off to infinity — some quickly, some after wandering for a while — but for others it stays bounded forever. Color every pixel of the plane by how many iterations it took to escape (and leave the never-escaping points a fixed dark color) and you get the Mandelbrot set: exponent 2 is the classic shape, exponent 3+ gives the "multibrot" variants with more-fold symmetry. Flip z and c — start z at the pixel and fix c at one chosen point (the Julia knobs) — and the same recurrence traces the Julia set for that point instead, a different picture for every c.

  
The coloring is Palette Synth's cosine-gradient formula, reused rather than reinvented: escape speed becomes t, bands and offset rescale it before it's fed through a + b·cos(2π(c·t+d)), and the palette picker borrows that instrument's own six presets directly. Escape-time and color are computed separately on purpose — dragging a view or fractal knob recomputes the expensive part (iterating every pixel), while Animate and the color controls just redraw the already-computed field through a shifting palette, which is why the picture keeps moving smoothly even though the fractal itself hasn't changed.

  
The picture itself is also a control, not just a display: drag on it and, in Mandelbrot mode, it pans the view; in Julia mode, it sets c to whatever point is under the pointer — a 2D pad generalized down from a single drag point. Either way it's driving the same View and Fractal knobs, not a separate code path, so the knobs and the drag never disagree.
