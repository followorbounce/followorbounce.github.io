How it’s counted

Rows and squares

Each row starts on that year’s birthday, so the grid never drifts: 52 weeks of 7 days make 364 days, and the last square of each row absorbs the remaining one or two days. A 29 February birthday is counted on 1 March in common years. The grid is a picture: the numbers beside it are counted from the calendar itself — weeks lived are whole 7-day weeks since your date of birth, so they run about 1.2 weeks a year ahead of the grid’s 52 squares.

The average line

World life expectancy at birth was about 73 years in 2023 (UN World Population Prospects 2024). It is an average over everyone born, not a forecast for you — it varies widely by country, sex and decade of birth.

Exact figures

Days alive, weeks and weekends come straight from the calendar: days are counted between dates (so daylight-saving changes never shift them), weeks are whole 7-day weeks, and a weekend is counted by its Saturday — lived up to and including today, left after today until your 100th (or 120th) birthday. Full moons use the mean synodic month (29.530589 days); distance around the Sun uses Earth’s mean orbital speed, 29.78 km/s.

Body estimates

Heartbeats (70 a minute), breaths (15), blood pumped (5 litres a minute), sleep (8 hours), blinks (15 a minute while awake) and meals (3 a day) are rough adult averages applied to every day. Children’s hearts and lungs run faster, so treat them as order-of-magnitude.
